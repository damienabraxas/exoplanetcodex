A new model atom of nitrogen was developed using the energy levels o f N I from lab-
oratory measurements and also predicted in atomic structure calc ulations and the most
up-to-date atomic data for computing radiative and collisional rate s of the transitions.
Solar abundance log ε⊙,N(1D NLTE) = 7.92 ±0.03 was determined from lines of N I by
applying the synthetic spectrum method with the plane-parallel (1D ) MARCS model at-
mosphere and taking into account the departures from local ther modynamic equilibrium
(non-LTE eﬀects). Having implemented the 3D-corrections of Ama rsi et al. (2020), we
obtained for the Sun log ε⊙,N(NLTE+3D) = 7.88 ±0.03. Based on high spectral resolution
spectra, non-LTE abundances of nitrogen were derived for 11 un evolved A–F type stars
with reliable atmospheric parameters. Non-LTE leads to strengthe ned N I lines, and the
non-LTE eﬀects grow with increasing eﬀective temperature. For e ach star, non-LTE leads
to smaller abundance error compared to the LTE case. For superﬁ cially normal A-type
stars, non-LTE removes the enhancements relative to the solar n itrogen abundance ob-
tained in the LTE case. A λ Boo-type star HD 172167 (Vega) also has close-to-solar N
abundance. The four Am stars reveal a scatter of the N abundan ces, from [N/H] = −0. 44
to [N/H] = 0.39. The N abundances of the Sun and superﬁcially normal A stars are con-
sistent within 0.09 dex with the nitrogen abundance of the interstella r gas and the early
B-type stars.

1. Introduction
Nitrogen is one of the three most abundant elements (C, N, O) afte r hydrogen and
helium. It plays an important role in the physics of stars, and knowled ge of its abundance
in a particular star, or rather, the change compared to the initial a bundance at the birth
of the star, is the key to understanding the evolutionary status o f the star and the physical
processes occurring in it. One believes that the chemical compositio n of galactic matter
changed very little after the formation of the Solar System, there fore, for most chemical
elements, their meteoritic abundances serve as abundance stand ards for the present-day
Galactic matter or as cosmic abundance standards. But nitrogen is a volatile element,
and the cosmic abundance standard for N is determined by the Sun a nd young, unevolved
stars.
In the solar spectrum, nitrogen is observed in a few, weak and blend ed lines of
N I, as well as in the molecular lines of NH and CN. For many years, the abu n-
dance log ε⊙,N = 8 . 05 ± 0. 04 obtained by Anders and Grevesse (1989) from molecular
lines using the semi-empirical solar model atmosphere of Holweger an d Mueller (1974)
was considered as standard. Hereafter, we use the abundance s cale where log εH =
12. With the same model atmosphere, but from the N I lines, Rentzsch-Holm (1996)
determined log ε⊙,N = 8 . 05 ± 0. 09 based on the non-local thermodynamic equilibrium
(non-LTE, NLTE) line formation. In the early 2000s, 3-dimensional (3D) atmospheric
models based on hydrodynamic calculations became widespread. Usin g the 3D model
and non-LTE abundance corrections (diﬀerences between non-L TE and LTE abundances:
∆ NLTE = log εNLTE − log εLTE) for the N I lines calculated with the 1D model, Caﬀau et al.
(2009) derived log ε⊙,N = 7.86. In self-consistent 3D and non-LTE calculations for N I,
Amarsi et al. (2020) determined the lower abundance, log ε⊙,N = 7.767, but a higher value
of log ε⊙,N = 7.89 (3D) was obtained from the NH and CN lines (Amarsi et al., 2021) . It is
necessary to understand the reasons for such discrepancies be tween diﬀerent authors and
between atomic and molecular lines.
For A-type stars, non-LTE abundances from the N I lines were derived by Takeda
(1992); Lemke and Venn (1995); Rentzsch-Holm (1996); Przybilla and Butler (2001);
Lyubimkov et al. (2011) and in the later studies based on already dev eloped non-LTE
methods. The methods were tested on the star Vega (HD 172167) with reliable atmospheric
parameters and a high-quality observed spectrum. In various wor ks, non-LTE corrections

following tasks were set.
1. Construction of a new model atom for nitrogen to be applied to bo th solar type
and A-type stars. The model atom should be as complete as possible and provide a close
collisional coupling of the N I high-excitation levels to the ground state of the next stage
ionization N II.
2. Determination of abundances from the N I lines in the solar spectrum. We already
have experience in determining the solar elemental non-LTE abunda nces using a classi-
cal one-dimensional (1D) model of the solar atmosphere from the MARCS model grid
(Gustafsson et al., 2008), and our results are in excellent agreeme nt with self-consistent
3D and non-LTE calculations of Asplund et al. (2021). Namely, Alexee va and Mashonkina
(2015) obtained the same abundance from lines of C I and molecular lines of C 2 and CH:
log ε⊙,C = 8. 43 ± 0. 02, which is by 0.03 dex less than that in Asplund et al. (2021). With
the same atmospheric model, Sitnova and Mashonkina (2018) derive d the non-LTE abun-
dance from lines of O I, log ε⊙,O = 8 . 70 ± 0. 08, which is consistent within 0.01 dex with
that in Asplund et al. (2021).
3. Determination of non-LTE abundances from the N I lines in the selected stars of
F, A and late B-type. This is an extension of our previous detailed stu dies of the chemical
composition of these stars. The results of non-LTE abundance de terminations for He, C,
O, Na, Mg, Si, Ca, Ti, Sr, Zr, and Ba are summarized by Mashonkina et al. (2020) and
Romanovskaya et al. (2023). Sitnova et al. (2022) and Mashonkina (2024) determined the
non-LTE abundances of Zn and Sc.

2. Method of non-LTE calculations for N I
2.1. New model atom of nitrogen
Energy levels. The atomic model is built using 321 energy levels of N I from the
NIST1 database (Kramida et al. 2019) and 503 levels predicted by Kurucz ( 2014) in the
atomic structure calculations for N I, but not detected (yet) in laboratory measurements.
The excitation energies of the highest levels are less than the ionizat ion energy of N I
by 0.08-0.26 eV, which is much smaller than the average kinetic energy of electrons at
temperatures up to 20 000 K. This ensures eﬀective collisional coup ling of N I with the
ground state of N II. Multiplet ﬁne structure was neglected. Highly excited levels with low
energy separation ( <250 cm −1) and of the same parity were combined into superlevels.
They are formed predominantly from levels predicted in calculations o f the N I atomic
structure. The average energy of the combined superlevel was c alculated taking into
account the statistical weights of individual levels. The ﬁnal atomic model includes 59
levels of N I. Singly ionized nitrogen is represented only by its ground state. Exc ited levels
increase the partition function of N II by about 5% at T = 10000 K. Test calculations
have shown that taking into account excited levels of N II has a negligible eﬀect on the
magnitude of deviations from LTE for levels of N I. The model atom is shown in Fig. 1.
Radiative rates. gf -values for 46 109 bound-bound (b-b) transitions between levels
used in constructing the atomic model are taken from calculations b y Kurucz (2014). After
combining the levels, we got 743 b-b transitions with the oscillator str ength f > 10−6. For
transitions from the ground state 2 p3 4 S◦ and low-excitation levels 2 p3 2 D◦, 2 p3 2 P◦, in
which UV pumping of the upper levels can occur, radiative rates are c alculated using the
Voigt absorption proﬁle (in total, for 16 transitions). For other tr ansitions, the absorption
proﬁle is the Doppler one.

sections calculated in the Opacity Project (Seaton, 1987) are tak en from the TOPbase

2.2. Statistical equilibrium of nitrogen depending on eﬀec tive temperature
To solve the system of statistical equilibrium (SE) and radiative tran sfer equations in
a given atmospheric model, we use a modiﬁed code detail (Giddings, 1981; Butler, 1984;
Przybilla et al., 2011). We note that the line opacity is calculated direct ly, line-by-line
using the list of lines by Kurucz (1993), which includes about 40 million line s.

with diﬀerent eﬀective temperatures ( Teﬀ ). Here, nNLTE and nLTE are level populations,
obtained by solving the SE equations (non-LTE) and using the Boltzm ann-Saha formulas
(LTE). Throughout this work we use plane-parallel (1D) model atm ospheres. For the Sun,
the model with Teﬀ = 5780 K and surface gravity log g = 4.44 (5780/4.44) is taken from
MARCS database 2 (Gustafsson et al.2008). The models 9550/3.95 and 10400/3.55 wer e

In an atmosphere with Teﬀ = 5780 K, neutral atoms dominate in the nitrogen abun-
dance, therefore the ground and two lowest excited levels keep th ermodynamic equilibrium
populations and reveal b = 1 throughout the atmosphere. The lowe r level 3 s 4P of the
transitions, in which the observed lines are formed (see Table 1), is o verpopulated (b > 1)
above log τ5000 = 0 due to radiative pumping from the ground state in the wings of the N I
1199.55, 1200.22, and 1200.71 ˚A lines. The upper levels, e.g. 3 p 4S◦, are depopulated (b
< 1) in spontaneous transitions to the lower states. As a result, lines of N I are enhanced
compared with the LTE case. But the departures from LTE are wea k.
In the 9550/3.95 model atmosphere, N I remains the dominant ionization stage, but
radiative processes are strengthened compared to collisional one s, and the departures from
LTE are stronger than in the case of the solar atmosphere. Not on ly the lower, but also
the upper levels of the transitions, in which the observed lines are fo rmed, are overpopu-
lated, but to a lesser extent than the lower ones. Therefore, non -LTE leads to increased
absorption in the lines.
In the highest temperature model (10400/3.55), neutral and sin gly ionized nitrogen
have comparable number densities. For the low-excitation levels 2 p3 2 P◦ (λ thr ≃ 1130 ˚A)
and 2 p3 2 D◦ (λ thr ≃ 1020 ˚A), the intensity of the ionizing radiation exceeds the local
value of the Planck function resulting in overionization of these levels . Due to their close
coupling to the ground state, the latter is also emptied (b < 1). Radiative pumping from
the ground state leads to enhanced population of the high-excitat ion levels in such a way
that, in each of the transitions associated with the observed lines, blow > bup (the lower
and upper levels of the corresponding transitions are indicated in Ta ble 3), and the lines
are strengthened compared to the LTE case.

3.1. Analysis of the N I lines in the solar spectrum
We analyze the spectrum of the Sun as a star using the Atlas of Kuru cz et al. (1984).
Spectral resolving power is R = λ/ ∆ λ ≃ 520 000. As in our previous studies, we use the
canonical parameters of the solar atmosphere: Teﬀ = 5780 K, log g = 4.44, microturbulence
velocity ξt = 0.9 kms −1 and the classical atmospheric model from the MARCS database.
Abundances from the N I lines were determined by the synthetic spectrum method,
that is, by automatically ﬁtting the theoretical spectrum to the ob served one. The syn-
thetic spectrum was calculated with the code synthV NLTE (Tymbal et al., 2019),
integrated into the BinMag visualization program (Kochukhov, 2018 ). Using b-factors
from the code detail, synthV NLTE calculates lines of N I taking into account the
non-LTE eﬀects, while lines of other elements are computed under t he LTE assumption.
Line atomic parameters for calculating the synthetic spectrum are taken from the cur-
rent version of the database V ALD3 (Vienna Atomic Line Database, Pakhomov et al.,
2019). For the N I and CN molecular lines, we adopted gf -values from calculations by
Tachiev and Froese Fischer (2002) and Brooke et al. (2014), resp ectively. In our calcula-
tions, we used a ﬁxed value of the solar carbon abundance, log εC = 8.43, as derived by
Alexeeva and Mashonkina (2015).
In the solar spectrum, there are no unblended (pure) lines of N I. Five lines, which
are most suitable for determining N abundance, are listed in Table 1. F our of the ﬁve

– 8216.8 ˚A, using EW in abundance determinations is unjustiﬁed.

3.2. Comparisons with literature
Our abundance from atomic lines agrees within 0.02 dex with log εN(3D+NLTE) =
7.86±0.12 obtained by Caﬀau et al. (2009). They performed the 3D LTE ca lculations for
lines of N I and then implemented the non-LTE corrections from calculations wit h a 1D
model atmosphere. In the non-LTE calculations, inelastic collision wit h hydrogen atoms
were treated using the Drawin (1969) approximation and applying a s caling factor SH. In
the solar atmosphere, the non-LTE eﬀects for N I lines are small, and our ∆ NLTE values
are consistent within 0.01 dex with the corresponding values of Caﬀa u et al. (2009, Table
4), if SH = 1/3 is adopted. Caﬀau et al. (2009) used slightly diﬀerent gf -values compared
to the data of Tachiev and Froese Fischer (2002). Conversion to n ew gf -values changes
the average abundance by less than 0.01 dex.
The diﬀerence with Amarsi et al. (2020) for the abundances, obta ined in 1D non-
LTE calculations with the same atmospheric model as in this work, has already been
noted above. In self-consistent 3D non-LTE calculations, Amarsi et al. (2020) determined
log εN(3D NLTE) = 7.77 from ﬁve lines of N I. For each line, they calculated a less
negative non-LTE correction (∆ NLTE ≃ − 0. 01 dex) compared to ours. Therefore, the
lower abundances in their work are mainly due to using equivalent width s and a physically
incorrect procedure for subtracting the calculated equivalent wid ths of the CN lines from
the observed equivalent widths of the N I + CN blends.

4.4. Comparisons with literature
Of all the sample stars, Vega has largest set of nitrogen non-LTE a bundance deter-
minations in the literature. In all the works, very similar Teﬀ from 9500 to 9550 K and
log g from 3.9 to 4.0 were employed, so the diﬀerences in results cannot be explained
by diﬀerences in atmospheric parameters. Using the equivalent widt hs, even for the
N I lines in the hydrogen line wings, Takeda (1992) and Rentzsch-Holm (1 996) obtained
log εN ∼ 7.5 (solar metallicity model) and log εN = 7.76 (7.67 after applying the NIST
gf -values). Lemke and Venn (1995) and Przybilla and Butler (2001) us ed the synthetic
spectrum method and determined log εN ∼ 7.6 and log εN = 7.67. The use of the NIST
gf -values would change the average abundances in two latter papers by less than 0.01 dex.

5. Conclusions
A new model of the nitrogen atom has been constructed, which inclu des energy levels
of N I from laboratory measurements, as presented in NIST, and also en ergy levels pre-
dicted by R. Kurucz in the atomic structure calculations for N I. Radiative and collisional
rates for b-b and b-f transitions are calculated using the most up- to-date available so far
atomic data on photoionization cross-sections, electron-impact e xcitation cross-sections,
and rate coeﬃcients for inelastic processes in collisions with hydroge n atoms.
Non-LTE calculations for N I were carried out with the classical MARCS solar model
atmosphere. Non-LTE leads to strengthened lines of N I and negative non-LTE abundance
corrections, but the eﬀects are small, that is, ∆ NLTE does not exceed 0.05 dex, in absolute
value. Using the synthetic spectrum method, we derived the solar n on-LTE abundance
log εN(1D NLTE) = 7.92 ±0.03 from ﬁve atomic lines. Applying the 3D corrections for
individual lines from calculations of Amarsi et al. (2020), we obtained log εN(NLTE+3D)
= 7.88 ±0.03. This value is consistent with log εN(3D) = 7.89, obtained by Amarsi et
al. (2021) from the NH and CN molecular lines, and with log εN = 7.91, obtained by
Ryabchikova et al. (2022) from the CN lines and corrected for 3D eﬀ ects in accordance
with Amarsi et al. (2021). Our results are consistent within the err or bars with the solar
abundance log ε⊙,N = 7. 85 ± 0. 12, recommended by Lodders (2021).
Non-LTE calculations were performed for 11 unevolved F-A type st ars with reliably
determined atmospheric parameters. It is shown that negative no n-LTE abundance correc-
tions increase in absolute value with increasing Teﬀ , so that ∆ NLTE reaches −0. 48 dex for
N I 8683 ˚A in the model 10400/3.55. The nitrogen non-LTE abundances were determined
based on the observed high-quality spectra. For superﬁcially norm al A stars, non-LTE
removes the excess relative to the solar nitrogen abundance obta ined in LTE analysis. As
for elements from He to Fe, the nitrogen non-LTE abundance of th ese stars diﬀers from
the solar abundance by no more than 0.1 dex. A solar abundance was also obtained for
nitrogen in Vega, a star of the λ Boo type. Thus, we have expanded the list of elements in
a detailed analysis of the chemical composition of A-type stars – a pr oject whose results
were presented earlier by Mashonkina et al. (2020), Sitnova et al. ( 2022), Romanovskaya
et al. (2023), Mashonkina (2024). Four Am stars show a variation in nitrogen abundance,
from a deﬁciency of [N/H] = −0. 44 to an excess of [N/H] = 0.39.
